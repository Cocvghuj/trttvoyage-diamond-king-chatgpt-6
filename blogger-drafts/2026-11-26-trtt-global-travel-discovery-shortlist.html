How to Build a Travel Shortlist That Fits Your Journey

A practical way to compare destinations by region, interests and the kind of experience you want.

Planning a trip often starts with a simple question: where should I go? The answer depends on more than a trending photograph or a popular list. Your available time, interests, travel pace, budget, accessibility needs and entry requirements all matter.

TRTT Voyage created the Global Travel Discovery Explorer as a free starting point for exploring destination ideas. It lets readers filter a curated set of places by world region and travel style, search for a destination, and save a shortlist in the current browser tab.

1. Start with the experience, not the trend

Think about the kind of journey you want. Are you more interested in beaches and islands, culture and heritage, nature and landscapes, food, outdoor activities, city discovery or slow travel? A clear preference can make a broad search more manageable.

These categories are prompts, not labels that define an entire country. A destination may offer several different experiences, and the best fit depends on the specific region and your plans.

2. Compare regions before choosing a base

Countries and island groups are rarely one-size-fits-all destinations. Sardinia, for example, has different coastal areas, historic places and inland landscapes to explore. Portugal includes mainland regions as well as Madeira and the Azores. The Greek Islands are a diverse collection rather than a single uniform itinerary.

Use official destination portals to explore the regions that interest you, then narrow your plan to places that make sense for your dates and transport options.

3. Use a shortlist to make research more focused

Instead of opening dozens of tabs and trying to remember every option, keep a small shortlist. Compare each candidate against the same questions:

  • Does the destination match the experience I want?
  • Can I reach it within my available travel time?
  • What are the likely transport connections between areas?
  • Can I find current, reliable information about accommodation and local costs?
  • Does it meet my accessibility, dietary or other practical needs?

4. Verify the details before booking

A discovery tool is not a substitute for current travel checks. Before paying for flights or accommodation, confirm entry and visa rules with the relevant immigration authority for your passport, review your government’s current travel advice, and check weather information for your exact dates.

Also confirm transport schedules, accessibility arrangements, opening hours and booking conditions directly with official providers. These details can change, so do not rely on an old article or an inspiration shortlist as final travel advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Global Travel Discovery Explorer a booking website?

No. It is an inspiration and research-starting tool. It does not sell travel products or check live availability.

Does the shortlist save my choices permanently?

No. Selections remain in the current browser page session. Export the shortlist if you want a file to keep.

Does the explorer provide visa or safety advice?

No. Check official immigration authorities and current government travel advice for your circumstances and dates.

Are the listed destinations ranked?

No. The collection is selective and editorial. It is not a ranking or a claim that one destination is better than another.
