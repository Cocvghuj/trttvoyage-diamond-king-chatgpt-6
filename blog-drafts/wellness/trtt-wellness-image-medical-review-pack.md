## 1. Image production rules

Use original, non-explicit, respectful editorial imagery suitable for an adult educational website.

- Preferred output: landscape 16:9, with a clean focal point and generous negative space for responsive cropping.
- Brand palette: blush pink `#E98BAF`, muted berry `#B77991`, warm gold `#D6A542`, royal midnight `#11152D`, and white.
- Avoid nudity, sexualised poses, intimate acts, graphic anatomy, identifiable patient information, visible medical records, brand logos, misleading medical symbols, and text baked into the image.
- Represent adults inclusively without implying that one body type, gender, relationship structure or ability is universal.
- Do not depict a real identifiable patient or clinician without documented permission.
- For stock imagery, retain the original source URL, creator, licence type, licence date and any attribution requirement.
- For AI-generated imagery, record the tool, generation date and prompt; check the provider's current commercial-use terms. Do not describe AI-generated imagery as a photograph or as a real clinical scene.
- Never use an image that implies a treatment, diagnosis, product endorsement or health outcome not supported by the article.
- Compress appropriately, check mobile crop, and use descriptive alt text. Alt text should describe the image's relevant content, not repeat keywords.

## 3. Medical review protocol

**Reviewer qualification:** A currently licensed healthcare professional with relevant sexual and reproductive health expertise in the intended publication jurisdiction. Record reviewer name, qualification, registration/licence jurisdiction, review date and scope in a private editorial record. Do not publish personal contact details.

**Review standard:** The reviewer should assess factual accuracy, currency, clinical nuance, potential harm, symptom/urgent-care language, inclusivity and whether claims are appropriately supported. The reviewer must be free to request changes or advise against publication.

### Universal checklist — apply to all ten articles
- [ ] Scope and audience are clearly educational and adult-oriented.
- [ ] No diagnosis, personalised treatment instruction or promise of outcome is implied.
- [ ] Medical statements are accurate, current and proportionate to the cited evidence.
- [ ] The article distinguishes general information from individual clinical advice.
- [ ] Symptoms and risk language do not cause avoidable alarm or false reassurance.
- [ ] When to seek routine, prompt or urgent care is clinically appropriate.
- [ ] Sources are authoritative, relevant, current and support the adjacent statements.
- [ ] Terminology is respectful, inclusive and understandable.
- [ ] Consent, privacy, safeguarding and confidentiality statements are not overstated.
- [ ] Country-specific service pathways and emergency advice are checked for the intended audience.
- [ ] Reviewer comments have been resolved and final approval is documented.
